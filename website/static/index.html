Traffic event detection for one Tashkent intersection

Our entry for the WIUT Hackathon 2026 CV track. From a fixed 4K CCTV camera we detect jaywalking, cars not yielding at crossings, red-light runs, stop-line violations, stopped vehicles and congestion, and we output an accident-risk score for every frame. It runs offline: YOLO11 + ByteTrack for road users, the traffic light state read from the lamps in the image, and rules on the trajectories.

Problem and approach

Input: one .mp4 from a fixed camera. Output: [start_sec, end_sec, label] segments over 14 classes, scored by macro-F1 at temporal IoU 0.3 / 0.5 / 0.7. Separately, a causal risk score must rise before an accident. We have four unlabeled sample clips (no training labels for this camera), so we invest in things that generalise without labels: a strong pretrained detector, a tracker, an accurate model of this scene, and rules whose thresholds have physical meaning.

Blue outline = learned (pretrained weights, no fine-tuning); yellow = hand-written rules; grey = geometry and statistics measured from the sample videos.

Part A: event detection

  1. Decode smartly. ffmpeg (bundled static binary) decodes the 4K H.264 stream using all cores and drops to 5 Hz and 1280 px before Python sees a pixel. The two traffic-light heads are cut from the full-resolution frame in the same pass.
  2. Register the view. The framing differs between clips by up to ~3 % (re-aimed camera). A median background plate is matched to our reference view (SIFT on CLAHE-normalised images + RANSAC homography, ~5 s). All scene geometry lives in the reference view; lamp boxes are mapped into the video, and tracks are mapped back.
  3. Detect + track. YOLO11m (COCO) at 1280 px finds people, cars, buses, trucks, bikes and animals; ByteTrack links them into tracks.
  4. Kinematics. Each track becomes a ground-contact trajectory (box bottom-centre), smoothed, with speed measured in own box heights per second. That unit is roughly perspective-invariant, so one threshold works near and far.
  5. Signal phase. Lit red / green pixels in the two camera-facing signal heads, debounced over 1 s.
  6. Rules combine trajectories, the scene polygons, the learned flow field and the phase (table below).
  7. Segments are merged across short gaps, blips are dropped, and same-class overlaps are unioned (the organisers' convention).

Part B: accident anticipation (causal)

The estimator never opens the file and never touches Part A. It runs its own detector + tracker on every 6th streamed frame (5 Hz, 960 px) and scores:

  • Time-to-collision between road users: footprints (bottom 30 % of the box) extrapolated at constant velocity for up to 3 s. Only pairs that close faster than 0.8 heights/s count, so dense queues stay quiet.
  • Hard braking: speed lost within ~1 s.

Hazard -> logistic calibrated so normal traffic stays below the 0.5 alarm threshold, with a 1 s half-life decay.
